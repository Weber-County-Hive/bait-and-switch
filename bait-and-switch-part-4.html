What happened at 11:59 p.m. on March 6, 2026

Earlier pages in this project described several bills as "killed" by having their enacting clause struck at 11:59 p.m. on the last night of the 2026 session. That is accurate for each bill, but it leaves out something readers need to know: that is how the Legislature closes out every bill that has not passed.

We read the status record for all 1,004 bills and resolutions from the 2026 General Session on le.utah.gov. At 11:59 p.m. on March 6, 2026, the enacting clause was struck on 471 of them. At least 158 of those had already passed one chamber. At least 113 never had any committee action recorded.

So the strike itself is not a special kill. The question worth asking about any one bill is a different one: why did it never get a final vote? In both chambers, a bill reaches a floor vote by being placed on a calendar. A bill a committee sends back to the Rules Committee waits there unless it is moved out. The status records show where each bill stopped, but not why.

CASE 18 OF 19 · IN CONGRESS

A Trade Bill That Became the Government's Funding Bill

H.R. 6500 (119th Congress)

Utah's four House members, including Rep. Mike Kennedy, voted yes on the final version.

WHAT IT LOOKED LIKEThe "AGOA Extension Act," extending trade benefits for African countries. The House passed it 340–54 on Jan. 12, 2026.
WHAT THE RECORD SHOWSThe Senate's amended version, dated Aug. 8, 2026, carries the short title "Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027." The House agreed to it 370–48 on Sept. 1, 2026, and the President signed it Sept. 2. The White House described it as funding federal agencies through Dec. 11, 2026.

The trade extension did not disappear. Reporting on the signed law says it moved AGOA's end date from December 2026 to December 2028. What changed is that a bill introduced and passed as a trade bill became the vehicle for a short-term government funding law. The House Clerk's roll call for the Sept. 1 vote still labels it "AGOA Extension Act."

The timeline
  • Jan. 12, 2026House passed the AGOA Extension Act 340–54.
  • Aug. 8, 2026Senate's amended version: "Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027."
  • Sept. 1, 2026House agreed 370–48 (Roll Call 286). Utah members Kennedy, Moore, Maloy and Owens voted yes.
  • Sept. 2, 2026Signed into law.
C
Power: No public check removed; a common way Congress moves must-pass funding.
Transparency: Anyone following the bill by its name and number would not see it become the funding law, and the official vote page still carries the old name.

Correction to our earlier notes (Oct. 18, 2026): Earlier notes for the UT-4 case file described the funding bill as replacing the AGOA text entirely. Reporting on the signed law shows the AGOA extension stayed in.

CASE 19 OF 19 · IN CONGRESS

A New Name for a Delay

H.R. 9436 (119th Congress)

Sponsor: Rep. Mike Lawler (N.Y.), with Rep. Jared Golden (Maine)

WHAT IT LOOKED LIKEIntroduced June 24, 2026 under a plain title: "To amend the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023 to extend the time period for which certain regulations concerning the North Atlantic right whale are effective."
WHAT THE RECORD SHOWSThe committee replaced the text and gave it a name: the "Northeast Lobsterman Protection Act of 2026." The reported version changes "2028" to "2035," extending the pause on new right whale rules for lobster and Jonah crab fisheries by seven years. The House passed it 219–196 on Sept. 1, 2026.

We confirmed the new name and the 2028-to-2035 change in the reported text. We have not confirmed whether the introduced text already used 2035, so this case is about the name, not a change in substance.

–
Not graded. The name change is confirmed; a change in what the bill does is not.
